# A conserved piecewise-affine map with a nonsmooth onset of bounded chaos

**A note for external mathematical review — CYB-25**
**Referee target: `src/chaos/` (the recurrence and its measurement instruments).**

## 0. What this note is, and what it asks

This is a *mathematician-facing* statement of one dynamical object from the Cybeersym
project: a 21-dimensional, deterministic, conserved, **continuous piecewise-affine (CPA)**
map that exhibits a bounded chaotic attractor, born abruptly, coexisting with a
still-stable equilibrium, as one scalar parameter varies.

The note is deliberately narrow. It **defines the map directly from the implementation**,
states the invariants, and then **partitions every claim into three tiers** —
*proved / numerically evidenced / conjectural* — so that a reviewer can see exactly where
the numerical evidence stops and interpretation begins.

**What we are asking you to adjudicate** (expanded in §6):

1. Verify the recurrence in §2 against `src/chaos/model.py` — is the map as stated?
2. Challenge the conservation argument in §3 (the three unit eigenvalues / center
   subspace). Is the SFC functional exactly a λ=1 eigendirection ∀β, and is it exactly
   3-dimensional?
3. Challenge our use of the words **"attractor," "invariant," "chaos," "border-collision,"
   "bifurcation."** Which of these are earned, and which are numerical shorthand?
4. Adjudicate the central open question (§5): the onset is **not** a Nusse–Yorke
   boundary-equilibrium border-collision (we show why). What *is* the correct formal
   description of a nonsmooth onset of a coexisting attractor when the equilibrium is
   interior to every switching manifold and non-hyperbolic?

Out of scope for this note: the economic interpretation of the model, any forecasting
claim, and any new simulation. This is dynamics only.

## 1. The system in one paragraph

Three tiers (retailer, wholesaler, manufacturer) in series pass physical goods downstream
and orders upstream. Goods are **created only** by an infinite external supplier above the
manufacturer and **destroyed only** by constant consumer demand below the retailer;
between those two points goods are merely moved, on fixed-length transit delays. Each tier
sets its upstream order by an **anchoring-and-adjustment** heuristic (Sterman 1989) with
two nonnegativity/inventory constraints. Consumer demand is **constant** (`μ`, no noise):
every aperiodic trajectory below is generated by the deterministic map, not by stochastic
input. The single control parameter is the **supply-line weight** `β`.

## 2. The map, from the implementation

### 2.1 State

Fix `n = 3` tiers and integer lead time `L = 3`. Tier `i ∈ {0,1,2}` (0 = retailer,
2 = manufacturer) carries the real-valued state

$$
x_i \;=\; \bigl(I_i,\; B_i,\; O_i,\; \hat D_i,\; q_i^{1},\dots,q_i^{L}\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}^{4+L},
$$

where `I` = on-hand inventory, `B` = downstream backlog, `O` = *on-order* (supply line:
units ordered upstream and not yet received), `Ĥ = D̂` = adaptive demand forecast, and
`q_i^{1},…,q_i^{L}` = the FIFO transit line of goods in transit **toward** tier `i`
(`q_i^{1}` arrives next). The full state is the concatenation

$$
x = (x_0, x_1, x_2) \in \mathbb{R}^{(4+L)\,n} = \mathbb{R}^{21}.
$$

This is exactly `ChaosChain.get_state()` / `set_state()`; the map below is
`ChaosChain.step_vector`.

### 2.2 Parameters

`μ = 100` (constant demand), `θ = 0.25` (forecast smoothing), `S* = 100` (target net
stock), `a_S` (inventory-gap gain), and the control `β`, with the supply-line gain
`a_SL = β · a_S`. The primary route reported below fixes `a_S = 0.7`, `L = 3`, `θ = 0.25`
and sweeps `β`.

### 2.3 One step: a single sequential sweep `i = 0 → 1 → 2`

The update is **Gauss–Seidel**, not Jacobi: within one tick the retailer's freshly
computed order becomes the demand the wholesaler sees, and so on up the chain. Initialize
the demand seen by tier 0 as the constant consumer demand, `r_0 = μ`. Then for
`i = 0, 1, 2` in order:

1. **Receive** the arriving transit slot: pop `a = q_i^{1}`; set `I_i \mathrel{+}= a`,
   `O_i \mathrel{-}= a`. (The queue shifts; a new slot is appended in step 5/6.)
2. **Accrue demand:** `B_i \mathrel{+}= r_i`.
3. **Ship** downstream: `s = \min(I_i, B_i)`; `I_i \mathrel{-}= s`, `B_i \mathrel{-}= s`.
   - If `i = 0`: `s` leaves the system (consumed).
   - If `i > 0`: append `s` to the transit line of tier `i−1` (arrives after `L`).
4. **Update forecast:** `\hat D_i \mathrel{+}= θ\,(r_i - \hat D_i)`.
5. **Order** upstream (the nonlinearity):
$$
o_i \;=\; \max\!\Bigl(0,\;\; \hat D_i \;+\; a_S\,(S^* - (I_i - B_i)) \;+\; a_{SL}\,(L\,\hat D_i - O_i)\Bigr).
$$
   Then `O_i \mathrel{+}= o_i`.
6. **Propagate:**
   - If `i = 2` (manufacturer): append `o_i` to tier 2's own transit line (filled by the
     infinite external supplier, lag `L`); goods are **injected** here.
   - Else: set `r_{i+1} = o_i` — the order is the demand the next tier upstream sees, this
     same step.

Call the resulting map `F : \mathbb{R}^{21} \to \mathbb{R}^{21}`, `x_{t+1} = F(x_t)`.

### 2.4 Regularity of `F` — the one structural fact everything rests on

For **fixed** parameters, every operation above is affine in the state **except** the two
saturations `max(0, ·)` and `min(·,·)`. Both are continuous and piecewise-linear. Hence:

> **`F` is a continuous, piecewise-affine map on `\mathbb{R}^{21}`.**

Its switching manifolds are two families of hyperplanes, one pair per tier:

- **Order clamp** `H^{\mathrm{ord}}_i = \{\, \hat D_i + a_S(S^*-(I_i-B_i)) + a_{SL}(L\hat D_i - O_i) = 0 \,\}`
  — where `o_i` crosses `0` (a tier orders nothing).
- **Ship clamp** `H^{\mathrm{ship}}_i = \{\, I_i = B_i \,\}` — where `\min(I_i,B_i)`
  switches which argument is active.

The pieces are the polyhedral cells cut by these manifolds; on each cell `F` is affine.
This CPA structure is the reason the border-collision literature (Nusse–Yorke,
Banerjee–Grebogi, di Bernardo et al.) is the *natural* setting — and, as §5 shows, also
the reason its standard normal form does **not** directly apply here.

## 3. Invariants (the substrate)

### 3.1 Goods conservation (an exact accounting identity)

Because goods are created only at the manufacturer's inject and destroyed only at the
retailer's consume, the map satisfies, at every step,

$$
\text{injected} - \text{consumed} \;=\; \sum_i I_i \;+\; \sum_{i,k} q_i^{k}.
$$

This is structural (provable by construction of `F`). In simulation across the chaotic
regime it holds to a **relative residual ≤ 4.9 × 10⁻¹⁵** every step (`ChaosChain.step`
asserts it, with a scale-relative tolerance). Chaos here rides on an exactly conserved
substrate.

### 3.2 The supply-line functional and the center subspace

Linearizing `F` at the physical equilibrium (see §4), the full 21-D spectrum carries
**three eigenvalues numerically pinned at `λ = +1`, for every `β`**, ε-robust (identical
at finite-difference steps `10⁻⁴, 10⁻⁶, 10⁻⁸`). Their left eigenvectors are the per-tier
functionals

$$
\phi_i(x) \;=\; O_i \;-\; \sum_{k=1}^{L} q_i^{k}
\qquad(\text{on-order} - \text{total in-transit toward } i),
$$

i.e. the **supply-line accounting identity**. Stock-flow consistency appears as a
permanent **3-dimensional center subspace** of the equilibrium. Everything else in the
spectrum has modulus `≤ 0.92` and never touches the unit circle.

*(This is the crux we most want challenged: is `φ_i` provably a `λ=1` left-eigendirection
of `DF` at the FP for all `β`, and is the center subspace exactly 3-D? We have strong
numerical evidence and an analytic candidate, not a proof.)*

## 4. The measured phenomenon

All figures are in `docs/outreach/figures/`, regenerated by `src/chaos/run_v0.py` and
`run_route.py`. Parameters `a_S = 0.7, L = 3, θ = 0.25, μ = 100`, demand noise off,
initial condition a one-time offset off the equilibrium. `β` swept downward (smaller `β` =
more supply-line underweighting).

- **Equilibrium.** Initialized at the constant-demand fixed point, an unperturbed run sits
  still (both order gaps vanish, `o_i = μ`). The equilibrium is found by **iteration** in
  the stable regime and continued below onset by Newton; it stays **feasible and interior**
  to both switching manifolds through onset (every tier orders `μ`, far from the order
  clamp; stockout margin `≈ 129`, far from the ship clamp).
- **Onset.** As `β` falls, the equilibrium **does not destabilize** — the leading complex
  pair stays at `|λ| ≈ 0.91, ∠ ≈ 40°` and never reaches `|λ| = 1`. Instead, a
  finite-amplitude attractor appears **discontinuously**: the turbulent amplitude jumps
  `0 → ~525` over `Δβ ≈ 0.003`.
- **Coexistence / bistability.** In a `β`-window near onset, the turbulent attractor
  **coexists** with the still-stable equilibrium: a small initial offset decays to the
  fixed point; a large one lands on the turbulent set. (Endogenous path-dependence /
  hysteresis.)
- **The Lyapunov verdict.** The largest Lyapunov exponent (Benettin two-trajectory,
  `lyapunov.py`) is `λ ≈ 0` on the frequency-locked loop and turns **robustly positive,
  up to `+0.054` nats/step, below `β ≈ 0.26`**. The estimator is validated first on the
  logistic map (`r = 4 → λ = ln 2` to five digits) and returns `λ < 0` in periodic
  windows.
- **Sensitive dependence.** Two runs differing by `ε = 10⁻⁶` diverge `×10⁵` over ~200
  steps at `β = 0.15` (straight line on a log axis), and *converge* back to `10⁻¹¹` at
  `β = 0.32`. Same map, opposite verdict, set only by `β`.
- **Boundedness.** At `a_S = 0.7` the attractor's amplitude stabilizes (growth ratio ≈ 1),
  so `λ > 0` certifies chaos rather than blow-up. (A separate corner, `β → 0` at aggressive
  `a_S`, genuinely runs away — `λ > 0` on an *unbounded* orbit, which is **not** chaos and
  is excluded.)
- **Which border, and geometry.** On the developed attractor the dominant active
  constraint is the **order clamp** (`\max(0,·)` — the manufacturer orders zero
  **42–56 %** of steps), the ship clamp secondary (~24–27 %). Delay-embedded phase
  portraits show a closed invariant loop whose flat segments **ride the `o≥0` border**
  (the piecewise-smooth fingerprint), breaking down into a strange attractor; the power
  spectrum goes single-line → subharmonic at `f/2` → broadband.

## 5. The open problem: what kind of onset is this?

We attempted the formal classification (`bcb_classify.py`, `normal_form.py`, the latter
self-validated on three documented 2-D cases before use) and reached a **negative,
precise** result. The Nusse–Yorke / Banerjee–Grebogi **boundary-equilibrium**
border-collision normal form classifies a **hyperbolic** fixed point that **crosses** a
switching manifold as a parameter passes through 0. Here, for measured reasons, *none of
those hypotheses hold*:

1. **No boundary equilibrium.** The equilibrium is robustly **interior** to both manifold
   families for all `β` through onset — it never sits on a border to collide with.
2. **Non-hyperbolic equilibrium.** The `λ = 1` conservation triple (§3.2) means the FP is
   non-hyperbolic ∀β; nothing crosses the unit circle, so there is **no local bifurcation
   of the equilibrium at all** — not a smooth Neimark–Sacker / flip / fold, and not a
   boundary-equilibrium border-collision.
3. **The N-D → 2-D reduction is not sanctioned.** Projecting the 21-D map onto a dominant
   eigenplane and applying 2-D theory is not a theorem, and specifically fails for a
   **dominant complex pair** (Simpson et al. 2025: the only rigorous N-D robust-chaos
   reduction is to a 1-D skew tent map, requiring a dominant *real* eigenvalue). The
   one-sided Jacobians `J_L, J_R` at a candidate collision differ in **only two rows** (the
   manufacturer's `on_order`, a `λ=1` conservation direction, and one `λ=0` transit slot);
   the oscillatory plane is **identical across the border** (`|λ| = 0.945, ∠ 40°` on both
   sides), so any 2-D normal form built on the active mode sees **no collision**.

Our working description — offered as a **conjecture**, not a theorem — is:

> The onset is a **global, nonsmooth** event: a constraint-riding attractor is **born at
> finite amplitude and coexists** with the still-stable, interior, non-hyperbolic
> equilibrium. It is a border-collision (nonsmooth fold) **of the limit cycle**, not of
> the fixed point.

**The question for a mathematician:** is there a correct formal home for this? Candidates
we cannot ourselves adjudicate: a nonsmooth fold / saddle-node *of the invariant loop*;
a boundary crisis; a grazing-type bifurcation of the cycle against `H^{ord}`; or something
requiring machinery for **CPA maps with a persistent center subspace** that the standard
boundary-equilibrium theory does not cover. We suspect the SFC center subspace is not
incidental — the property that makes the map rigorous (conservation) is the same property
that places its equilibrium outside the standard classification.
