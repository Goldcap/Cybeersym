What the dials and axes mean

ωstatewage share (W/Y) — labour's slice of output; the distributional variable.
λstateemployment rate — fraction of the workforce with a job.
dstatedebt ratio (D/Y) — firm debt relative to output.
rglobalinterest rate on firm debt. Higher r drains output into interest and shrinks the safe basin — but does not move the local tipping point.
ksharplocalinvestment sensitivity — how sharply firms ramp investment as profit rises. The local-Hopf driver: turn it down toward the edge and the good equilibrium loses stability into cycles.
d₀globalinitial debt — an initial condition, not a parameter. It sets which basin you start in; the eigenvalue map cannot see it.
ρ, νstruct.Phillips-curve slope and capital-output ratio — structural constants (advanced).

What this shows (our reading — reviewer-gated)

The map colours an economy's fate: stable (disturbances fade), unstable (it generates its own cycles), and — for the chosen initial debt — breakdown (debt-deflation collapse).

The stable⇄unstable edge is a LOCAL tipping point (a Hopf bifurcation). Approaching it, the economy's response to a shock decays ever more slowly — the shock half-life lengthens. That rising persistence is a genuine, forecastable early warning (critical slowing down).

The breakdown from raising d₀ is a GLOBAL basin crossing. It changes an economy's fate without touching the map colour or the half-life — so there is no slow warning. Early warning works for the local tipping and is blind to the global basin; telling them apart is the contribution.

Division of labour: ksharp (with the structural terms) sets the local Hopf; r and d₀ govern the global basin. Verified against the numpy engine: the Hopf locus is r-independent.
